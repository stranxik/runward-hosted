## Context

The author's verdict of 2026-11-12 on the app: screens that scroll on and on, where a page should be fixed and only its elements scroll, with very little said. The mock-up answered with a fixed shell: one window whose bar says the screen in one line, a list and a detail, each pane scrolling on its own; on a phone the list, then the detail with its way back. The shell, its panes and Applications are lot 1 (ADR-0036). This record decides what lot 2 puts in those panes: the connect flow, the journal, Compte.

The same day, a defect was seen in production: a tab left open since the declaration, opened on `#/declarer/confirmer/<id>` once the first check had arrived (the status said `pendingReceivedAt`), said « Il n'y a rien à confirmer… Aucun envoi n'est encore arrivé » until the page was reloaded. The router read the access view once and kept it for every screen that read the same thing (its key was the same, `access`), so the flow showed what it had read at its load.

## Decision

1. **« Brancher une application » is five gestures, one action per step**, on the routes the app always had: (1) declare (`#/declarer`; `#/declarer/nouveau` on a phone, where `#/declarer` is the list), (2) add the step to the chain and (3) deliver, the first check awaited (`#/declarer/attente/<key>`), (4) confirm the first check and (5) read the first verdict (`#/declarer/confirmer/<key>`). The list beside it holds « Brancher un nouveau dépôt », the repositories in progress and those connected in the last 7 days.
   1. **Where a repository stands is read from the service** (`web/views/flux.js#etape`): the access view (its state, its kept first upload, its pins) and the status lines (an upload refused since the declaration, with its code). Its variants come from those states: « envoi non accepté » (step 3, the refusal said in the lexicon's words, with a link to its entry in the journal filtered for it), « déclaration expirée » (removed by the daily pass, 7 days with nothing received), a new owner or a GitLab definition to confirm (step 4).
   2. **« C'est fait, j'ai fusionné » is a marker of this screen only.** It moves step 2 to step 3 in this browser (kept per viewer, a convenience), it is never sent to the service, it proves nothing, and the service never reads it. What is pinned and what is published still wait for a real first upload and the member's confirmation by passkey; an upload that arrives moves the screen on, marker or not.
   3. **The waiting updates by itself, and every screen of the flow reads again at its entry.** The router reads the flow's views again each time one of its screens is entered (the arrival too), whatever was read before; while a screen of the flow is shown and the tab visible, the status is read again every 30 s as the portfolio does (ADR-0035), and the flow's reads are made again when its head moved. A waiting screen whose repository's first upload arrived moves to its confirmation by itself, and says so to assistive technologies. A member busy on the screen (typing, a confirmation box open, a passkey asked) is not repainted under their hands.
   4. **The rules do not change, only the presentation.** The consent is a box the member ticks, never ticked for them, and ticking it asks the passkey for that gesture (`declare-with-consent`, bound to the name declared); without it no upload is accepted. The level of evidence the forge chosen reaches is said before the repository is named and before the extract (ADR-0029 section 2). Mission, path, branch and workflow are folded with the service's own defaults (the mission is the repository's name as `src/domain/repository.js` writes it, the root, `main`, the forge's default file); a value the service refuses opens the fold on its field. The values a confirmation pins are shown before the gesture, which is bound to their digest (`repository-confirm`); a change of them since is refused (`confirmation-stale`) and the new ones show; « Ce n'est pas mon dépôt » removes the declaration as before. The token's verified claims are folded under the pins.
   5. **A first check confirmed and published shows the fifth gesture**: the first verdict (its state, its rail, its sentence) and « Ouvrir l'application », whose reading says it is the first (ADR-0035's « premier »). ADR-0035 point 4 said the confirmation opens the reading directly; the flow now ends on its own step, one click from it. A new owner confirmed, nothing published, a check set aside go where they went before.
2. **The journal is a list and a detail, filtered.** Newest first, one day at a time; the entry shown beside the list with every field the journal keeps, the refusal in the reader's words, the provenance folded. Its filters: an application (by its mission), a kind (uploads accepted, uploads not accepted, declarations and consents, confirmations, sign-ins, account gestures), a period (24 h, 7, 30, 90 days, a year). They are the service's: `GET /api/v0/journal?mission=&kind=&days=`, additive query parameters beside the cursor (`src/domain/journal-filter.js`): a filtered page reads the tenant's whole journal (the status line already does, every 30 s), keeps what the filter selects, pages by the same cursor, and its answer adds `filter` and `total`. Without a filter the answer is the one it always was. A value the service does not know is refused (`journal-filter-invalid`, 400), never ignored. Every act the service writes belongs to exactly one kind, held by a test. The filters and the entry shown are in the address (`#/envois[/<cursor>]?app=&quoi=&periode=&entree=`), so a link keeps them; the entry shown is not a read. Its states: nothing yet, filters that keep nothing (with the way to clear them), an entry the page does not hold (with the way to its page), an error.
3. **Compte is its sections as a list and the one chosen beside it**: passkeys, sessions, declared repositories and their consents, GitLab instances, language and automatic reader, members, « Récupérer vos données », what the service keeps; each row says what it holds now. The tasks (add a passkey, the export, the erasure, an instance, one repository) open in the detail pane, the list beside them with their section marked. The gestures are the same, with the same confirmation boxes, the same verification at each gesture, the same routes (`test/unit/liens.test.js`).
4. **The phone**: the list, then the detail with its way back; the step's one action at the bottom of the screen; targets of 44 px at least; no horizontal scroll (the filters scroll in their own line).
5. **The arrival counts every repository declared**, the ones waiting for their first check too (it said « 2 dépôts déclarés » for an account of 3).

## Alternatives discarded

- **The consent ticked by default**, as the mock-up drew it: a consent given by a box the member did not tick is not a consent, and it is the one gesture that lets content in.
- **Sending « c'est fait » to the service** (to date the merge, or to start a faster polling): it would be a word of the member's made to look like evidence; the first upload is the only proof, and it arrives by itself.
- **Filtering the journal in the page**: the journal is paged by 50; a filter over one page would say « no entry » while older pages hold some.
- **A store query per filter** (SQL over the entries' bodies): a new port method in both stores, for a journal the status line already reads whole every 30 s; to reconsider when a tenant's journal grows past what a whole read costs (the trigger below).
- **Polling the access view instead of the status**: three reads every 30 s instead of one; the status's head moves with every entry, so it says when to read the rest.

## Consequences

- **Positive**: one action per screen in the flow; the waiting needs no reload and moves on by itself; a tab left open never shows what it read at its load; the journal answers « what happened to this application this week »; Compte reads as a list of what it holds.
- **Negative, accepted**: entering a screen of the flow costs its reads each time (access, status, index: three, within the budget of architecture §6); a filtered journal reads the tenant's whole journal; the marker « c'est fait » is lost with the browser's storage, and the screen then shows step 2 again until an upload arrives.
- **On other boundaries**: the API contract gains one refusal (`journal-filter-invalid`); no route, field or answer of before changes (`test/unit/journal-filter.test.js`, `test/unit/http.test.js`, the forge port's pin of the refusals of before, `test/unit/forge.test.js` point 12). Nothing in identity, sessions, passkeys, the content security policy or the tenant's isolation.

## What would settle it

- **Confirms**: on the real service, a member declares, adds the step, merges, and the screen moves to the confirmation by itself within 30 s of the upload, confirms with the pins in view and lands on the first verdict; a journal filtered by an application and « uploads not accepted » shows the refused upload of that week, and the link keeps the filter; the author reads the three screens as the mock-up's.
- **Reverses**: the author wants the confirmation to open the reading directly again (point 1.5); a tenant's journal grows to where a filtered read is slow (then a store query).
